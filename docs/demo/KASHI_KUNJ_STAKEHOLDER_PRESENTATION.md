## Slide 1 — Title

**Sparkbuild Construction**
*Smarter Construction Management*

One platform to run every project — schedule, site, materials, money and
communication. (Live demonstration: **Kashi Kunj**, Jabalpur.)

## Slide 2 — The way construction usually works

- Plans live in spreadsheets, progress lives in someone's head, material
  surprises happen at the worst moment.
- Site photos go to WhatsApp, costs get reconciled weeks later.
- The owner finds out about delays *after* they happen.

**Result:** too much firefighting, too little control.

## Slide 3 — The way it should work

One dashboard for the whole company and every site:

- Every project's health at a glance — green, amber, red.
- One source of truth for the schedule, the site, materials and money.
- Alerts *before* small problems become big ones.

## Slide 4 — Meet Kashi Kunj (our demo project)

A 4-storey residential project in Jabalpur — 47% built, budget ₹90 lakh.

- Start 11 Jan 2026 · target handover Mar 2027
- 16 construction tasks · 8 site log entries
- 10 material lines · 5 suppliers · 3 invoices issued
- Four real users: Owner, Site Supervisor, Procurement, Client

## Slide 5 — One screen, every project

The Command Center shows all projects with their **computed** health.

- Kashi Kunj is **AMBER** today — and the system says why:
  progress is at 47% against 52% of the schedule elapsed.
- No one picked that colour. It is calculated from the actual data.

## Slide 6 — A schedule that thinks

- 16 tasks, linked like a real construction plan.
- Plan one task too early? The system **shifts it and tells the team** —
  automatically.
- Two tasks are overdue today; the dashboard flags them so the team can act.

## Slide 7 — Every day on site, captured

- Daily site logs: what was done, who was there, what was flagged.
- Recent log notes: *"Cement stock running low at site"* — a small note that
  becomes a real alert (next slide).

## Slide 8 — Materials without surprises

- 10 material lines, each with a live stock balance and a reorder level.
- **Cement is low today** (25 bags, reorder at 30) — the system raised an
  alert on its own.
- Every bag is on an unchangeable ledger: received, used, remaining.
  What the screen says is what the store actually holds.

## Slide 9 — Buying with controls

- Purchase orders move through a real workflow:
  draft → submitted → **approved/rejected** → received.
- Today: one PO waiting for approval, one rejected (rate too high — with a
  recorded reason), one arriving, one fully received.
- Approval is a **decision**, not a formality: only the owner can approve.

## Slide 10 — Money you can trust

- Budget: ₹90,00,000 · Spent: ₹32,98,150 · Remaining: ₹57,01,850.
- The number on screen equals the cost ledger — every rupee is traceable to
  a labour line, a material line, or a supplier receipt.
- No hand-typed totals. No "trust me".

## Slide 11 — Client confidence

- Clients see a clean portal: progress, schedule, and their invoices only.
- 3 invoices issued so far — 2 paid, 1 outstanding.
- Clients never see internal costs, suppliers or stock. They see what they
  should — nothing more.

## Slide 12 — The right view for the right role

- **Owner** — everything, every project.
- **Site Supervisor** — their site, schedule and logs. No money figures.
- **Procurement** — suppliers, orders, stock, finance reads.
- **Client** — progress and invoices.
- Verified by the system, not by good manners: wrong access is refused (403).

## Slide 13 — AI that helps — with guardrails

- Ask in plain language: *"What is low in stock?"*, *"Show open purchase
  orders."*
- When Copilot wants to **change** something (say, create a task), it asks
  for human approval first. We'll show one live — and reject it.

## Slide 14 — The proof is in the trail

- Every change is logged: who, what, when — an audit trail nothing can
  rewrite.
- Every alert (low stock, PO submitted, invoice issued) was raised by the
  system, not typed in.
- The same dataset can be reset and re-run identically — for rehearsal today,
  and for the real rollout tomorrow.
